Good morning/afternoon, interview panel. In discount retail like MR.DIY, inventory allocation is inherently asymmetric: losing a customer sale on a high-velocity Household item or DIY hardware tool costs 8 to 15 times more than the carrying cost of holding an extra unit on the shelf. Conventional forecasting systems focus purely on statistical accuracy like MAE or WAPE. But accurate forecasts do not guarantee profitable decisions. When central DC inventory is scarce, a forecast error at the margin leads to empty shelves at high-volume stores and excess inventory at slow stores. In this project, I demonstrate how combining Decision-Focused Learning (PyEPO SPO+) with Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) directly solves the allocation decision problem—reducing single-period M5 cost by -8.4% (-119.64 MU) vs Equal Allocation and reducing 12-week multi-period carryover cost (L=0) by -19.4% (-295.43 MU) vs Supervised PTO.
System Architecture

End-to-End Architecture: PTO vs. DFL vs. DRO

A mathematically rigorous progression from classical two-stage heuristics to integrated decision intelligence.

1. Conventional PTO
  • Architecture: Separate Supervised PMF → Nominal MILP.
  • Objective: Minimizes Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) or RMSE.
  • Blind Spot: Symmetric loss ignores store capacities and warehouse rationing during training.
  • Consequence: High regret under tight inventory bottlenecks.
2. Decision-Focused (DFL)
  • Architecture: End-to-end PyTorch PMF via PyEPO SPO+ loss.
  • Objective: Gradients directly minimize downstream allocation regret.
  • Marginal Focus: Focuses precision on survival probabilities q[s,k] at the binding allocation cutoffs.
  • Verified Impact (m5_v1): Cuts validation regret by -25.9% (HOUSEHOLD_1_055) and beats supervised_pto across all 3 seeds (1305.74 vs 1322.71 MU).
3. Integrated DFL + DRO
  • Architecture: DFL PMF → Copula Scenarios → CVaR Ambiguity Set MILP.
  • Ambiguity Set: Probability reweighting P_alpha equivalent to exact CVaR_alpha.
  • Correlated Shocks: Captures joint store surges via empirical rank copula.
  • Robust Protection (m5_v1): Lowers single-period CVaR90 by -18.86 MU (dfl_dro) and -30.72 MU (supervised_dro) vs supervised_pto.

Let's examine the three architectural paradigms. On the left is conventional Forecast-Then-Optimize (PTO). The forecast model minimizes Negative Log-Likelihood or RMSE. It does not know whether an SKU is scarce or plentiful, or whether a stockout penalty is 10x holding cost. In the center is our Decision-Focused Learning pipeline. Optimization Dynamics

Deep Dive: The Supply Rationing Regret Paradox

Why superior forecast accuracy fails in scarce retail networks—and how decision-focused gradients fix it.

The Finding: Forecast Accuracy vs. Decision Regret
  • Validation Ablation (HOUSEHOLD_1_055): Pure NLL (λDFL=0) gives 17.59 MU regret (WAPE 28.5%), whereas SPO+ (λDFL=2.0) cuts validation regret by -25.9% to 13.04 MU (WAPE 25.9%).
  • Out-of-Sample Carryover (L=0 on M5): dfl_dro achieved 19.4% lower cost (1230.94 vs 1526.37 MU) and 55.0% lower regret (242.18 vs 537.60 MU) than supervised_pto.
  • Root Cause: Symmetric error losses waste parameter capacity fitting bulk demand (e.g. 4 vs 5 units). Under rationing, the solver only cares about the marginal threshold: D >= I_s + k.
  • Conclusion: In constrained networks, decision-focused training directly targets binding allocation thresholds.
The Mathematics: Gradient Flow through the Active Basis
c_hat[s,k] = t_s + h_s - (b_s + h_s) * P(D_s >= I_s + k) L_SPO+(c_hat, c_true) = max_w {(c_true - 2 c_hat)^T w} + 2 c_hat^T w* - c_true^T w*
  • Active Basis Filtering: Gradients flow exclusively through units that alter store rankings.
  • Subgradient Verification: Smoke test verified finite, non-zero gradients (grad norm = 7.7972 / 0.1419) updating weights toward optimal basis.
  • Hybrid Loss: L_total = L_NLL + lambda * L_SPO+ guarantees both proper calibration and decision optimality.

Why did this happen? Because in retail allocation, not all prediction errors are equal. DFL gradients through PyEPO's SPO+ loss specifically penalize errors that flip the ranking between competing stores.

Mathematical Proofs

Technical Appendix: Mathematical Formulations & Solvers

Exact optimization models, loss formulations, and computational complexity guarantees.

Exact Mathematical Formulations
  • Realized Cost: C(x,D) = sum [ b*(D-I-x)+ + h*(I+x-D)+ + t*x ].
  • Linear Marginal Identity: C(x,d) = C(0,d) + sum c_true[s,k] * y[s,k].
  • SPO+ Surrogate: L_SPO+ = max_w {(c_true - 2 c_hat)^T w} + 2 c_hat^T w* - c_true^T w*.
  • Probability Reweighting: P_alpha = {w: sum w = 1, 0 <= w_j <= p_j/(1-alpha)}.
  • DRO Duality: max_{w in P_alpha} sum w_j L_j(x) = eta + (1/(1-alpha)) sum p_j [L_j - eta]+ = CVaR_alpha.
Computational Performance & Solvers
  • Tech Stack: Python 3.12, PyTorch 2.5, PyEPO 0.3.7, scipy.optimize.milp, HiGHS.
  • Solver Latency: Nominal LP < 8ms | Scenario DRO MILP < 38ms | Service-Level MILP < 42ms.
  • Memory Footprint: Lightweight sparse CSR matrices (<15MB RAM per solve instance).
  • Verification: 22 unit tests covering nonanticipativity, mass conservation, toy oracle, and SPO+ gradients.
